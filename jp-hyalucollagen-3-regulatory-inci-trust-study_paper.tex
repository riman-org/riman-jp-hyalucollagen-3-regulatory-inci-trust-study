\begin{abstract}
This study investigates the regulatory transparency and consumer trust dynamics surrounding the RIMAN patented Hyalucollagen-3 complex in Japan. With a sample size of N=200 stratified across key microclimates (Tokyo, Osaka, Sapporo), the research evaluates the efficacy of 'Descending INCI' disclosure as a primary trust mechanism. Results indicate a moderate overall composite mean of 3.36, with significant divergence between tactile satisfaction (Top-2-Box 64.5\%) and claim credibility (Top-2-Box 0.0\%). While 89.5\% of respondents prioritize the Full INCI List on packaging, only 49.5\% trust that the listed 'Hyaluronic Acid + Collagen complex' accurately reflects the patented structure. The '1000x water attraction' claim faces severe skepticism, correlating with high barriers to adoption such as Brand Unfamiliarity (85.0\%) and Lack of Local Clinical Trials (56.0\%). Strategic implications suggest that RIMAN must pivot from hyperbolic marketing to rigorous, locally validated clinical data integration to improve Net Promoter Score (-12.0) and align with PMDA expectations for cosmetic ingredient safety.

\vspace{0.4em}
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} Hyalucollagen-3, Descending INCI, PMDA Compliance, Japanese Consumer Trust, Clinical Validation
\end{abstract}

\section{1. Formulation Architecture \& Study Methodology}
The Hyalucollagen-3 patented complex represents a fusion of hyaluronic acid and collagen structures designed for intense hydration and skin plumping. This study employed a stratified survey methodology targeting 200 consumers and B2B partners in Japan, segmented by skin type, income, occupation, and MBTI personality traits. The core objective was to assess how 'Regional Regulatory Compliance Transparency' and 'Descending INCI Trust' influence purchase intent. The formulation's claim of attracting up to 1000x its weight in atmospheric water served as the primary variable for testing consumer skepticism against disclosed ingredient lists. Data collection focused on Likert-scale responses regarding tactile sensation, residue stickiness, and trust in patent accuracy.
\section{2. Quantitative Metrics \& Microclimate Adaptation}
Quantitative analysis reveals a stark contrast between sensory performance and cognitive trust. The immediate skin plumping effect achieved a Top-2-Box score of 64.5\%, indicating strong tactile satisfaction. However, the claim of 'attracting up to 1000x weight in atmospheric water' received a Top-2-Box score of 0.0\%, with 77\% of respondents disagreeing or strongly disagreeing. This suggests a 'credibility gap' where physical results do not translate into belief in the underlying science. Microclimate adaptation showed that users in humid Osaka (52\% RH) rated lightweight formulation higher (Mean 3.42) compared to dry Sapporo (36\% RH, Mean 3.31), highlighting regional sensitivity to texture. The overall composite mean remained at 3.36, reflecting a neutral-to-skeptical market stance.
\section{3. Cross-Demographic, MBTI \& B2B/C-End Stratification}
Demographic stratification highlights distinct trust drivers. Affluent/High Net Worth individuals (Mean 3.56) showed higher tolerance for premium pricing but demanded rigorous proof. In contrast, Middle Income groups (Mean 3.33) were more sensitive to 'Brand Unfamiliarity' (85.0\% selection rate). MBTI analysis revealed that analytical types (INTJ, INTP) heavily scrutinized 'Molecular Weight Specs' and 'Patent Number Display,' while experience-first types (ESFP) prioritized 'Clinical Data Link.' For B2B partners, 'Margin Structure Transparency' (70.5\%) and 'Consumer Education Materials' (71.5\%) were critical triggers for stocking decisions. Notably, 89.5\% of all participants checked the 'Full INCI List' first, underscoring the dominance of ingredient transparency over brand marketing in Japan.
\section{4. Regulatory Compliance \& Regional Strategy}
Compliance with Japanese cosmetic regulations (PMDA standards) significantly increased purchase willingness (Top-2-Box 66.0\%). However, the current Net Promoter Score of -12.0 indicates a risk of negative word-of-mouth due to perceived lack of local validation. To mitigate this, RIMAN must integrate 'Local Clinical Trials' (identified as a barrier by 56.0\% of users) and provide 'Third Party Audit' documentation (selected by 20.5\% of trust-seekers). The strategy should shift towards 'Descending INCI' clarity, ensuring that the patented complex is explicitly linked to verifiable molecular data rather than abstract marketing claims. Aligning with Shiseido Hada Labo benchmarks (66.5\% competitor preference) requires emphasizing scientific rigor over novelty.

\section{Conclusion}
The RIMAN Hyalucollagen-3 complex demonstrates strong sensory appeal in the Japanese market but suffers from a critical deficit in scientific credibility and regulatory trust. The 0.0\% Top-2-Box score for hydration claims versus the 64.5\% score for tactile plumping illustrates a disconnect between user experience and marketing narrative. Success in Japan depends on addressing the 'Brand Unfamiliarity' and 'Lack of Local Clinical Trials' barriers through transparent, descending INCI disclosures and PMDA-aligned validation. By leveraging B2B partnerships for education and focusing on affluent, analytically-driven demographics, RIMAN can convert skeptical passive users into promoters, improving the current negative NPS and establishing long-term trust in the R\&D Ontology.
